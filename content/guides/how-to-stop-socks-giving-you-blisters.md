# How to stop your socks giving you blisters

Most foot blisters come down to one thing: friction. Skin rubbing against a sock or shoe, again and again, until the layers of skin separate and fill with fluid. Heat and moisture make it worse, because damp skin is softer and tears more easily.

Healthdirect's advice for preventing friction blisters is simple: wear shoes that don't rub, and wear moisture-wicking socks to keep your feet dry ([healthdirect, blisters](https://www.healthdirect.gov.au/blisters)). Here's how to put that into practice.

## Why socks cause blisters

Your sock is the layer between your skin and your shoe, so it either reduces friction or adds to it. Socks make blisters more likely when they:

- **Bunch or wrinkle**, creating a ridge that rubs the same spot all day
- **Slip down at the heel**, so the shoe rubs bare skin
- **Hold moisture**, softening your skin
- **Have thick toe seams** that press into your toes
- **Are the wrong size**, too big (bunching) or too small (pressure points)

## 1. Keep your feet dry

This is the big one. Damp skin blisters far more easily than dry skin.

- Choose **moisture-wicking socks** rather than ones that hold sweat against your skin. Bamboo is a good option here; we compare it with the most common alternative in [are bamboo socks better than cotton](/guides/are-bamboo-socks-better-than-cotton).
- On long days or hot days, **carry a spare pair** and change at lunch.
- If your feet sweat a lot, read our guide to the [best socks for sweaty feet](/guides/best-socks-for-sweaty-feet).

## 2. Get the fit right

A sock should sit smooth against your foot with no loose fabric at the toe or heel.

- **Heel in the right place.** If the heel of the sock sits up on the back of your ankle, the sock is too small. If it slides under your foot, it's too big.
- **No slipping.** Ankle socks that disappear into your shoe leave your heel exposed to the shoe counter. We've got a whole guide on [why ankle socks slip off](/guides/why-do-ankle-socks-slip-off).
- **Smooth toes.** Check the toe seam. It should be flat, not a thick ridge.

## 3. Match sock height to your shoe

The sock needs to be taller than the shoe where the shoe touches your skin.

- **Low trainers:** a proper ankle sock that sits just above the shoe line at the heel.
- **High-top sneakers, work boots, hiking boots:** a crew sock. See [crew vs ankle socks](/guides/crew-vs-ankle-socks) for which suits what.

## 4. Break in new shoes slowly

New shoes are the classic blister trigger. Wear them around the house or for short trips first, and don't debut them on a long walk or a big day on your feet.

## 5. Protect hotspots early

A hotspot is the warm, tender feeling before a blister appears. Stop and deal with it straight away. Healthdirect suggests covering friction hotspots with hypoallergenic adhesive tape or using an anti-friction balm ([healthdirect](https://www.healthdirect.gov.au/blisters)).

## If you already have a blister

Leave it intact if you can, and cover it with a dressing to protect it. Get advice from a pharmacist or GP if it looks infected (red, hot, swollen or weeping). If you have diabetes or poor circulation, don't treat foot blisters yourself; see a health professional. We also have a guide on [bamboo socks for diabetics](/guides/bamboo-socks-for-diabetics) covering what to look for in a sock.

## The quick checklist

- Moisture-wicking socks
- Snug fit, heel in the right spot, no bunching
- Sock taller than your shoe
- Spare pair for long or hot days
- New shoes broken in slowly
- Tape hotspots early
